In plain English

What a household policy is.

A short set of rules your family agrees for its money, the way a pension fund or a family office writes down how it invests. It answers a few plain questions: what the money is for, how long it could wait, how much to keep in each kind of investment and how much cash to keep at hand.

Why it matters

Rules on paper beat decisions in the moment.

Calm decisions, not rushed ones

Rules agreed on a quiet day keep a bad week in the markets, a tip over dinner or a sales call from deciding for you.

Drift is caught early

What does well quietly grows into a bigger share than you ever chose, and cash runs down without anyone deciding it should. Finna shows it the same day, while it is still an easy call.

Everyone works from the same page

Your partner, your advisers and, later, your children can see what was agreed and why, so nobody has to guess what you would have wanted.

Your rules. Your decisions.

Finna gives you the questions and does the checking. You set every number, and Finna never suggests one.

  • Nothing moves on its own. When something slips outside a rule, you are told the same day. Finna never moves your money; what happens next is your family’s decision.
  • Nothing is written over. A change is saved as a new version, dated and agreed by the people it binds, so you can always see what the rules were when a decision was made.
  • No advice. Finna gives no investment, tax or legal advice. When a question needs an expert, Freya says so, and you bring in the one you choose.
